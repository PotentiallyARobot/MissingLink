✦ IMAGE STUDIO

Edit any image by describing the change

Upload a photo and write what should be different. The model keeps everything you didn't mention — the same face, the same lighting, the same composition — and changes only what you asked for.

  1. Open the Edit tab and drop an image in, or click to browse.
  2. Write the change in plain language: "remove the background", "make it night time", "put her in a red coat".
  3. Optionally add a negative prompt for things to avoid, or hit AI Suggest to have one written for you.
  4. Press Generate. The result lands on the stage — send it back as input to keep editing, or share it to the community gallery.
WRITE LIKE THIS Describe the end state, not the operation. "A cracked stone wall behind her" works better than "change background". Name what must stay: "keep her face and pose exactly the same."

🎥 CAMERA VIEWS

Any angle of the same subject

Orbit a virtual camera around a single photo. The subject stays consistent while the viewpoint moves — useful for character sheets, product shots, reference sets, and anything that needs to look like the same thing from six directions.

  1. In the Camera tab, upload your subject.
  2. Set the pose with the 3D orbit widget, or the azimuth / elevation / distance controls under it.
  3. For one shot, press Generate. For a full set, choose Multi-view — 6 or 20 angles in one pass.
  4. Hit Configure Views to edit exactly which angles you get, and to give each view its own prompt. You can export the whole config as JSON and re-import it later.
CONSISTENCY Multi-view runs every angle from your original image, so shots don't drift. Use a clean, well-lit source with the subject fully visible for the best results.
100 tokens per view

▦ BATCH

One instruction. Up to twenty images.

Load a set, write one edit, and it applies to every selected image — with per-image planning so the same instruction adapts to what's actually in each picture.

  1. Open Batch and drop in multiple images — or pull them from your history with the + button, or search the web for references.
  2. Tick the images you want. Selected tiles are outlined in gold.
  3. Pick your engine: Qwen (full LoRA control), SDXL (checkpoints + Civitai LoRAs), or GPT.
  4. Write one instruction and press Generate. Tiles fill in as each render finishes; any failure can be retried on its own.
BATCH TOOLS Upscale runs an enhance pass over every selected image. Audit Consistency flags mismatches across a set. Caption for LoRA writes training captions and downloads them as a zip.
100 tokens per image · 50 on SDXL

✧ CREATE

Start from nothing but a sentence

No source image required. Describe what you want and get up to four variations, optionally guided by reference images you supply.

  1. Open Create and write your prompt. Be specific about subject, setting, lighting, and style.
  2. Add up to 4 reference images if you want a particular character, object, or look carried through.
  3. Choose output size — square, portrait, landscape, or a custom aspect — plus quality and format.
  4. Set how many variations you want (1–4) and press Create.
REFERENCES BEAT ADJECTIVES One reference image of the character you mean is worth a paragraph of description. Drop it in a reference slot and mention it in the prompt: "the woman from the reference image, now on a rooftop at dusk."

◈ LORAS

Style adapters, stacked to taste

LoRAs are small add-ons that push a model toward a style, subject, or skill. MissingLink ships several built in, and lets you pull any compatible one from Civitai.

  1. In Camera, Edit, or Qwen Batch, open the LoRA sliders in the sidebar. 0 means off; around 1.0 is a normal working strength.
  2. Tap the ⓘ next to any LoRA for what it does, its trigger phrase, and a recommended weight.
  3. On the SDXL engine, use Browse Civitai to search checkpoints and LoRAs, stack several, and set each one's weight.
  4. Check the LoRA library in the community gallery — every entry is labelled with the engine it runs on (Pony, SDXL, Illustrious, Qwen).
ENGINE MATCHING MATTERS A Pony LoRA won't work on Qwen, and an SDXL LoRA won't work on Pony. Match the badge to the engine you've selected. Stacking too many at full strength causes smearing — pull weights down to 0.5–0.8 when combining.

⟳ NEXT SCENE

Continue the story, shot by shot

Chain generations into a cinematic sequence. The first shot comes from your image; every shot after that grows out of the one before it, so the world stays coherent while the camera and the action move.

  1. In Camera, upload your opening image and open Configure Views.
  2. Tick 🎬 Next Scene chain. Every view's prompt gets the "Next Scene:" trigger the model was trained on.
  3. Write each row as the next shot: "the camera pushes through the doorway, revealing the living room."
  4. Save and generate. Shots render one at a time, each feeding the next.
DRIFT IS REAL Because each frame is built from the last, small artifacts compound down a long chain. Keep sequences to a handful of shots, and run an Upscale pass on any frame that softens before continuing from it.

🎞 VIDEO STUDIO

Turn a still into motion

Image-to-video: hand it a picture, describe the movement, and get a short clip. Works beautifully on anything you made in the Image Studio — or anything on the community wall.

  1. Open Video Studio and upload your starting frame.
  2. Describe the motion — what moves, and how the camera behaves.
  3. Pick your settings and render. Finished clips appear in your history and can be shared to the gallery like any image.
MOTION, NOT SUBJECT The still already establishes what things look like. Spend your prompt on movement: "hair drifting in the wind, slow push in" beats re-describing the character.

🧊 3D MODELS

A textured 3D model from one photo

Generate a downloadable mesh from a single reference image — for games, prints, mockups, or just seeing your creation from every side.

  1. Open 3D Models and upload one clear photo of your subject.
  2. Choose your output settings and generate.
  3. Preview the result, then download the model file to use in your own tools.
BEST SOURCE IMAGES One object, centred, plain background, even lighting, nothing cropped off the edges. Busy scenes and partial subjects produce the weakest meshes — a Camera-view pass first can give you a cleaner source to feed in.

✦ AI AGENT

Ask for the whole job, not each step

Every studio has an agent panel. Tell it what you want in one sentence and it plans the work — generating, editing, filtering, and organising your batch for you.

  1. Open the Agent panel and describe the outcome: "generate 4 dragon concept sketches" or "make every image in the batch look like winter".
  2. Put anything it should reference into a shared context slot — those images are attached to every generation it runs.
  3. Ask it to search the web for reference images and it will pull them straight into your batch.
  4. Watch the queue as it works. You can stop or clear jobs at any point.
BE OUTCOME-SHAPED "Four variations of this character in different weather, keep her outfit consistent" gives the agent everything it needs. Vague asks get vague plans.

Common questions

Which engine should I use?

Qwen is the default for editing real photos — it preserves what you didn't ask to change. SDXL is for stylised work and when you want Civitai checkpoints and LoRAs. GPT is strong at following complicated written instructions and at text inside images.

Why did my result come out smeared or mushy?

Almost always too many LoRAs, or one at too high a weight. Check every slider — anything you left switched on from an earlier session still applies. Drop weights to 0.5–0.8 when stacking, and remember that chained sequences compound softness with each shot.

Can I use what I make commercially?

Your generations are yours. Note that individual Civitai LoRAs and checkpoints carry their own licences — check the model page on Civitai if commercial use matters for your project.

What happens when I share to the gallery?

Your image appears on the community wall under your handle, where others can like, comment on, and tip it. You can unshare at any time from your history.

Do my images stay private by default?

Yes. Nothing is public until you explicitly share it to the gallery.
